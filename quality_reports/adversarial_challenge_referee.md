## Major concerns

### Concern 1: The theory's unit of analysis and the method's unit of analysis are not the same thing

**Dimension:** 3 (also 1) · **Severity:** MAJOR

This is the mandated methods interrogation, and it is what I would lead with as a referee.

Read the research question's verbs: actors **justify**, **construct compromises**, justifications **come into dispute**. Every one is interactional and situated. That is not incidental — it is what Boltanski and Thévenot's apparatus is *about*. Worth is established in a **test** (*épreuve*): a bounded episode with actors, objects and a stake, in which a claim is put under critique and either holds or fails. A compromise is not an attitude someone holds; it is a fragile arrangement built around a specific composite object in a specific dispute, which the paper itself correctly reports (§3b, via Trevisan and Mouritsen) can be reopened by renewed critique. Critically, B&T reject the model of a person carrying stable values across situations. Their central competence claim is that the *same actor* shifts orders as the situation shifts. Nobody "is" a market person.

Q methodology delivers the opposite object. A participant sits alone with forty cards and produces one decontextualized preference ordering; by-person factor analysis aggregates those into person-level viewpoints. The paper's own §5 illustrations make the reification visible: "a market-oriented viewpoint," "a civic, redistributive viewpoint." That is precisely the stable-value-holder model the theory denies. Q can show that certain ranking patterns cluster. It cannot show a justification being offered, tested, defeated, or compromised — because no dispute occurs anywhere in the procedure.

**The steelmanned alternative:** naturalistic study of real data-sharing negotiations — the paper's own Future Research item #2. Negotiation transcripts, successive contract drafts, board minutes and steering-committee recordings contain the object EW theorizes: claims advanced, critiques raised, objects invoked as proof, compromises assembled and reopened. Investments of form would be observable *in use* rather than inferred from stated values. A referee will note, uncomfortably, that the authors have correctly identified the method matching their theory and deferred it, while running the mismatched method first.

Two lesser alternatives outperform Q on specific sub-questions. For RQ3's trade-off content, a discrete-choice experiment (their Future Research #1) yields quantified marginal rates of substitution rather than ordinal within-person patterns. For any hybrid-versus-commercial comparison, a Likert survey supports population inference; Q does not (Concern 5).

The objection is not unanswerable. Q's real defense is that it surfaces the *repertoires* actors can draw on — the justificatory vocabulary available to them — without claiming those repertoires are situationally stable, and that the post-sort interview restores some situatedness. That defense is nowhere in the manuscript, and it substantially narrows what the study may claim.

**What would change my mind:** A methodology subsection that (a) states explicitly that factors are interpreted as *available justificatory repertoires*, not person-types or held value positions; (b) rewrites the §5 viewpoint labels and the RQ verbs to match that narrower claim; and (c) either grounds each sort in a specified concrete data-sharing decision the participant actually faced (making the sort situated), or cites Q-methodological work defending the abstract-sort design against exactly this situated-versus-dispositional critique.

### Concern 2: A Q-set structured by the theory guarantees the theory is recovered

**Dimension:** 3 · **Severity:** MAJOR

§4 states the forty-statement Q-set is distilled by "theoretical content analysis, to ensure systematic sampling across orders of worth and the value-creation and value-capture distinction." The instrument is deliberately built to span eight orders crossed with the creation/capture dyad. RQ1 then asks "which orders of worth... characterize each" viewpoint.

That is close to circular. Factor analysis recovers structure present in the item set. If statements are sampled to represent market, industrial, civic, domestic, inspired, fame, green and project worth, then factors loading on recognizable subsets of those orders is close to a design guarantee, not a finding. The hedge — "this alignment is anticipated rather than assumed in advance" (§5) — describes an expectation, not a safeguard.

A second problem compounds it: eight orders × two value moments is a sixteen-cell frame carrying forty statements, roughly 2.5 per cell. Thin for claiming systematic coverage, and it means one badly-worded statement can carry an entire order.

**What would change my mind:** A stated falsification condition — what pattern of factor results would count as evidence *against* orders of worth being the right frame for data value? Plus at least one of: a Q-set with a substantial block of statements generated inductively from the interviews and *not* pre-coded to any order, so an unanticipated grammar of worth can surface; or a documented double-coding exercise in which independent coders assign the final forty statements to orders, with disagreement reported, so the coding is shown non-arbitrary rather than asserted.

### Concern 3: The case that Economies of Worth beats the alternatives is asserted, not argued

**Dimension:** 1, 2 · **Severity:** MAJOR

This is the mandated theory interrogation. The paper's entire defense against the strongest rival is two sentences (§2, §3b) resting on a single 2013 citation (Cloutier and Langley): institutional logics is "less well suited to the microfoundation justificatory work," EW is "complementary rather than competing." That is exactly a settled-without-justification move.

**The steelmanned objection**, in the order a referee would deploy it:

*First, institutional logics owns this question.* The paper motivates the study with hybridity — Battilana and Lee on organizations reconciling commercial and social-mission logics — then abandons that literature for the analysis. How hybrids manage competing logics, and by what mechanisms they hold them together, is the core question of a large active stream (Pache and Santos on selective coupling and individual-level responses; Besharov and Smith on logic compatibility and centrality). Why is the framework supplying the paper's own definition of its population not the framework used to analyze it?

*Second, the microfoundations argument is a decade stale.* Treating "institutional logics lacks microfoundations" as current in 2026 on a 2013 citation is untenable — Thornton, Ocasio and Lounsbury's own model works through attention and vocabularies of practice, and there is a substantial subsequent practice-level and individual-level literature. The claim may be defensible in a narrow form; not in this form.

*Third, a sharper rival exists for the paper's own mechanism.* The most interesting claim here is that some worth claims dominate because they are better instrumented. The commensuration literature — Espeland and Stevens on commensuration as a social process — theorizes precisely how a common metric absorbs and eliminates rival value dimensions, and is not cited. Nor is the valuation-studies stream that has already worked on data specifically (assetization of personal data, the making of data commodities), nor Stark's heterarchy, which applies orders of worth to organizations sustaining multiple evaluative principles — organizationally much closer to this population than Fourcade on environmental damage or Sharon on digital health.

*Fourth, the paper builds up service-dominant logic and then drops it.* §3a develops value-in-use, co-creation and Grönroos at length. SDL already holds value to be beneficiary-determined, contextual and plural. The reader is never told what EW adds that value-in-use cannot supply.

The honest answer to all four is **investments of form** — no rival supplies the concept of a durable, standardized proof device that makes a claim defensible in a dispute. That is a real and specific advantage. It is also the argument the paper does not make.

**What would change my mind:** A paragraph naming institutional logics, service-dominant logic and commensuration as live alternatives and stating, for each, the specific question about data value it *cannot* answer that EW can — with investments of form doing the work. Replace "complementary, not competing" with an actual comparison. Update the institutional-logics claim against post-2013 microfoundations work, or narrow it to what still holds.

### Concern 4: The only novel mechanism rides on the least-developed component — and is flagged as droppable

**Dimension:** 1 · **Severity:** MAJOR

Strip out the instrumentation-asymmetry finding and what remains is a descriptive typology of viewpoints on data value. Domain extension. Top-tier OT and IS journals reject "framework X applied to domain Y" absent a theoretical twist, and the twist here is RQ4.

Three problems converge. First, RQ4 carries an inline comment — "provisional — revisit before final draft; may be dropped or folded into RQ2/3." The distinguishing contribution is marked optional. Second, the paper concedes the Q-sort cannot answer it (§4) and assigns it to post-sort interviews plus "a document analysis of the proof devices actually used in participating organizations' data-sharing agreements and reporting" — with no sampling frame, no coding scheme, no analysis procedure, no document count and no access plan, despite data-sharing agreements being routinely confidential. Third, and most damaging: the expected finding is that market and industrial orders are heavily instrumented while civic, domestic, inspired and green are not. That is close to what Thévenot's original account implies and close to what Fourcade and Sharon — both cited — already found in adjacent domains. The paper would be confirming a known asymmetry in a new setting.

**What would change my mind:** Promote RQ4 to the paper's spine and give the document analysis a real methods paragraph — document types, expected N, access route, coding scheme for identifying a proof device, and how instrumentation "weight" is assessed rather than asserted. Then state what is *new* beyond Fourcade and Sharon. My candidate is the §5 observation that civic and green worth have well-developed proof devices in other domains (certifications, ESG reporting) that have **not migrated into data-sharing practice**. Non-migration of an existing instrument is a genuinely new, checkable claim. Make that the contribution and the paper changes character.

### Concern 5: Q design parameters stated as settled where the Q literature actively contests them

**Dimension:** 3 · **Severity:** MAJOR

Direct hit on the critical peeve. §4 specifies "principal components extraction with varimax rotation, retaining factors with eigenvalues greater than 1" with no justification. Within Q methodology all three are contested, and the standard modern reference (Watts and Stenner) recommends against each: centroid extraction with judgmental rotation over PCA/varimax, and explicit rejection of the Kaiser-Guttman eigenvalue>1 criterion as inappropriate for Q. These are defensible choices; they are not defaults, and presenting them as defaults will read to a Q-literate reviewer as unfamiliarity with the method's own debates. Same for the forced quasi-normal distribution, whose claimed benefits ("sharpens critical engagement and reduces the tendency toward neutral responses") are asserted without citation and are themselves the subject of a long forced-versus-free distribution argument.

Separately and more seriously, there is an inference error. The 2:1 hybrid-to-commercial ratio is justified because ten participants "comfortably exceeds the conventional four-to-eight-participant floor needed for a distinct viewpoint to emerge within the commercial contrast group." This treats the P-set as a sample supporting subgroup comparison. Q's P-set is non-probabilistic by construction; factor loadings carry no information about prevalence in any population. The study can say a viewpoint *exists* and is coherent. It cannot say commercial actors hold it more than hybrid actors do — which is what "contrast group" implies. If subgroup comparison is genuinely wanted, that argues for a survey instrument, not a differently-sized P-set.

One further contamination issue: a subset of the twelve concourse interviewees is re-invited to sort. The disclosure is commendable but is presented *only* as a disclosure. Participants who helped author the statements are ranking their own language.

**What would change my mind:** Justify extraction, rotation and retention against the Q methodological literature, or switch to centroid with defensible retention criteria (Humphrey's rule, or two-plus significant loaders). Cite the forced-distribution claim. Rewrite the 2:1 rationale to drop prevalence language — commercial participants widen the range of viewpoints surfaced; they do not license a between-group comparison. State how many concourse interviewees also sort, and how their sorts are handled in interpretation.

### Concern 6: The ontological premise contradicts the paper's own theoretical commitment

**Dimension:** 3 · **Severity:** MAJOR

§1 and §3a adopt Castro Fernandez's separation of data, "whose value is objective, proportional to the precision of what it represents," from documents, whose value is task-dependent. The paper then builds its case on Alaimo et al. and Nabben, for whom value is relationally and socio-technically constructed all the way down, and on EW, in which worth is never a property of an object independent of a test. These commitments are incompatible, and the paper adopts the first as a settled premise in order to claim the second as its opening. A constructivist reviewer will not accept "data's value is objective" as given, nor that a framework holding worth to be constituted in justification can be bolted onto an objectivist substrate without argument.

**What would change my mind:** Either defend the objective/subjective split as a substantive position and reconcile it with the relational commitments, or use Castro Fernandez purely as motivation — an example of the field's tendency to seek a single scale — without adopting his ontology.

## Minor suggestions

- The main RQ is ungrammatical: "and how they construct compromises" should read "and how they construct."
- §4 is a single ~700-word paragraph carrying the entire research design. Break into concourse, Q-set, P-set, administration, analysis.
- Two open TODOs sit in load-bearing places: remaining expert interviewees, and the exact grid range. Grid shape is not a detail — it determines the extremity of the distinguishing statements.
- At least ten of thirty participants come from the Torino Social Impact ecosystem. "Not confined to one country" is true but undersells a one-third concentration in a single regional ecosystem, which bounds the viewpoints that can surface.
- Treating all social-economy organizations (cooperatives, mutuals, associations) as hybrid by category flattens real variance in how central and compatible the two logics are within any given organization.
- `Bibliography_base.bib` marks every EW, Q-methodology, hybrid-organization and value-capture entry as an unverified STUB. The §3a data-valuation entries are verified; the theoretical and methodological backbone is not. Resolve before circulating externally.
- §5's expected results are specific enough to read as pre-registered predictions. Consider stating them as such explicitly — that converts a weakness (findings announced in advance) into a strength.

## Positive observations

**Investments of form is the right piece of the apparatus, and using it as an explanatory mechanism is the paper's real idea.** Most organizational applications of Economies of Worth stop at the taxonomy of orders and use it as a value-pluralism vocabulary — the generic gloss. This paper reaches past the taxonomy to Thévenot's 1984 argument about proof devices and asks a *why* question with it: not merely that justifications differ, but that they differ in defensibility because they differ in instrumentation. That is genuine engagement with what the framework claims, it is under-exploited in the organizational literature, and it is what distinguishes EW from every rival named in Concern 3. My strongest recommendation is to build the paper around it rather than treating it as a provisional fourth research question.

**The observation that civic and green worth are well-instrumented elsewhere but un-instrumented for data-sharing specifically** (§5) is the most publishable sentence in the draft. Instrument non-migration across domains is a mechanism, it is checkable, and it is not something Fourcade or Sharon establish.

**The creation/capture dyad as an organizing axis is a smart design decision.** Predicting consensus on creation and divergence on capture gives the study a real chance of a non-obvious, falsifiable result, and it grounds an abstract framework in a concrete empirical distinction.

**The §3a literature synthesis is well curated and current**, and the draft is intellectually honest where it did not have to be — the concourse/P-set overlap disclosure, the "anticipated rather than assumed" hedge, and the explicit concession that the Q-sort alone cannot answer RQ4. That last admission is what led me to Concern 4, but a paper that surfaces its own weak joint is easier to fix than one that hides it.
