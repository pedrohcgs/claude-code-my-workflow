# Academic writing

Write for a careful journal reader. Lead with the claim, then provide its support.

## Voice

- Prefer active voice and concrete subjects.
- Hedge exactly to the evidence: causal language requires an identification argument; descriptive or correlational evidence does not.
- Split sentences that carry multiple hard ideas.
- Avoid throat-clearing, stacked transitions, intensifiers, canned contrasts, symmetric list rhythms, and unnecessary em dashes.
- Prefer plain, specific words over abstract or promotional language.

## Evidence discipline

- Define notation at first use and keep it consistent.
- Give quantitative claims their units, uncertainty where applicable, and a source such as a table, figure, equation, or citation.
- Keep one main point per paragraph and make the opening sentence state it.
- Attribute prior findings to specific sources. Never use a citation that has not been checked.
- Do not invent citations, numbers, results, quotations, or metadata. Use visible placeholders such as `[CITE]` or `[NUM]` when support is missing.

## Revision

Preserve the author's substantive meaning and established voice. Tighten unsupported claims, surface assumptions near the claims they license, and flag any passage whose correctness cannot be established from the provided materials. Use `$humanize` for a dedicated AI-voice audit and `$verify-claims` for factual verification when requested or warranted.
