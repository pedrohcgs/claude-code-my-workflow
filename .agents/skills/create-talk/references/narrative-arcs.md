# Narrative arcs by paper type

Use these as sequencing logic, not mandatory section titles. The talk should
follow the paper's actual contribution and the audience's decision problem.

## Reduced-form

**Puzzle → identifying variation → credible estimate → implication**

Make the estimand and comparison legible early. Show the design in plain
language before an equation, pair the main estimate with its magnitude and
uncertainty, then devote scarce main-deck space to the threats the audience is
most likely to challenge. Put specification catalogs in backup.

## Structural

**Question requiring a model → motivating facts → mechanism → identification
and fit → counterfactual and welfare**

Explain what the model makes possible that a reduced-form analysis would not.
Build the economic environment across complete slides rather than overlay
states. Map parameters to data variation, show fit that is informative about the
counterfactual, and state where the counterfactual extrapolates beyond support.

## Theory plus empirics

**Mechanism → distinguishing prediction → test → evidence → scorecard**

Make predictions stable and numbered. Pair each important prediction with the
test and result that bear on it. End with where the theory succeeds, fails, or
needs qualification rather than presenting only confirming evidence.

## Descriptive or measurement

**Measurement problem → construction and validation → striking fact → changed
understanding**

Treat construction and validation as part of the contribution. Show one
high-value fact at a time with population and units, then explain why existing
measures missed it and what new questions the measure enables. Avoid causal
language unless a separate design supports it.

## Survey experiment

**Decision or belief problem → intervention → randomized contrast → treatment
effects and heterogeneity → interpretation**

Show the treatment materials when appropriate, clarify the assignment and
estimand, and keep manipulation checks, attrition, and multiple-outcome handling
visible enough to interpret the evidence.

## Formal theory

**Puzzle → primitives → mechanism → central result → intuition → implications**

Minimize notation to what the talk needs. State the assumptions that license
the central result and separate theorem intuition from proof detail. Put proofs
and auxiliary lemmas in backup unless the venue specifically evaluates them.

## Rhythm across types

Alternate evidence-dense slides with sparse synthesis or transition slides.
Show the main payoff by the point at which the audience has enough design or
model context to interpret it—normally no later than the middle of the planned
speaking time. Short talks should carry one primary result and one implication.
