# Full-draft checkpoints

Checkpoints protect substantive decisions; they are not arbitrary word-count
gates. A user may approve a different sequence.

## Framing checkpoint

Before sustained drafting, show:

- one-sentence question and contribution;
- paper type and proposed section architecture;
- closest-literature positioning;
- authoritative sources for notation, citations, and numbers; and
- unresolved author decisions.

Pause when this would establish or materially change the paper's framing.

## Design checkpoint

After the setting/data and strategy/model sections are coherent, reconcile them
with the approved strategy memo and analysis implementation. Surface changes in
sample, estimand, variable definition, identifying assumptions, inference, or
model-data mapping before using those sections to frame results.

## Evidence checkpoint

Results and empirical conclusions require:

- actual saved outputs, not predicted signs or placeholder examples;
- a trace from each reported number to its display and generating code;
- verified units, samples, uncertainty, and specification labels; and
- visible unresolved or failed checks.

If these prerequisites are missing, draft a clearly labeled results plan or
skeleton rather than prose that reads as a completed finding.

## Artifact checkpoint

Compile or render from the authoritative source. Check unresolved citations and
cross-references, display placement, generated-fragment integrity, and visible
layout. Proofreading and substantive review operate on this artifact, not on an
assumed successful build.
