# Paragraph argument moves

Name a paragraph's job before drafting it. Combine moves only when their logic
is genuinely inseparable.

## Motivation

Move from a concrete fact, tension, or decision to why it matters and then to
the unresolved question. Avoid generic importance claims.

## Contribution and literature positioning

State what the closest checked work establishes, the exact difference in this
paper, and what that difference changes. Citations support a comparison; they
are not a list of papers read.

## Identification preview

Name the data variation, estimand, and central assumption in plain language
before formal notation. State the evidence that makes the assumption credible
and the most important threat.

## Result statement

Lead with direction, magnitude, units, population, and uncertainty. Then explain
economic or substantive meaning and compare with a benchmark if that comparison
is supported. Point to the display after stating the finding.

## Mechanism

State the proposed channel, observable implication, and test. Distinguish
evidence consistent with a channel from evidence that identifies it uniquely.

## Robustness or falsification

Name the threat first, then the check and what its result does to the claim.
Avoid cataloguing specifications whose only rationale is that the coefficient
stays similar.

## Qualification

State the population, setting, design, model, or measurement boundary and why it
matters. A limitation should narrow a claim, not perform ritual modesty.

## Transitions

Use transitions to make logical dependence visible. Delete sentences that only
announce the next section without explaining why it follows.
