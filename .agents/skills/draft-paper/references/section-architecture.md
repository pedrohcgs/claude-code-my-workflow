# Paper-type-aware section architecture

Treat these as argument maps, not fixed headings or word counts. The target
venue, approved manuscript plan, and project's actual contribution take
precedence.

## Shared backbone

An empirical paper usually needs to make five relationships legible:

1. the question and why resolving it matters;
2. the contribution relative to the closest work;
3. what data or model make the question answerable;
4. what assumptions license the result; and
5. what was learned, with uncertainty and scope.

The introduction should preview the actual design and principal finding only
when verified evidence exists. A roadmap is optional and should not substitute
for an argument.

## Reduced-form

- Introduction: puzzle, question, design and estimand preview, verified main
  finding, and precise literature contribution.
- Setting/data: institutional treatment process, sample, outcomes, timing,
  comparison units, and measurement limitations.
- Empirical strategy: identifying variation, estimand, specification,
  assumptions, inference, and threats.
- Results: preferred estimate, magnitude, uncertainty, dynamic or heterogeneous
  effects, and threat-linked robustness/falsification evidence.
- Conclusion: what the design establishes, where it does not generalize, and
  implications that do not outrun the estimate.

## Structural

- Introduction: question that requires a model, mechanism, data/estimation
  preview, verified counterfactual, and contribution.
- Motivating facts/data: moments and variation connected to model primitives.
- Model: agents, timing, information, choices, constraints, equilibrium, and
  mechanisms.
- Identification/estimation: parameter-to-variation map, normalizations,
  estimator, computation, and uncertainty.
- Results: parameter interpretation, fit using evidence not mechanically
  targeted where possible, counterfactual support, welfare, and sensitivity.
- Conclusion: policy implications conditional on model structure and explicit
  extrapolation limits.

## Theory plus empirics

- Make the mechanism and distinguishing predictions explicit before the tests.
- Number or otherwise stabilize predictions so each empirical test maps to one.
- For each prediction, state the test, evidence, result, and whether it supports,
  qualifies, or contradicts the theory.
- Report failures and boundary conditions rather than narrating only confirming
  predictions.

## Descriptive or measurement

- Motivate the construct or empirical fact and why existing measures miss it.
- Treat data construction, linkage, validation, and sensitivity as core
  contributions rather than boilerplate.
- Organize results around a small set of numbered facts, each with magnitude,
  uncertainty, and population scope.
- Keep correlational evidence noncausal unless a separate design licenses a
  causal interpretation.

## Survey experiment

- Describe sampling, randomization, treatment materials, outcomes, estimands,
  attrition, and analysis timing.
- Present manipulation and balance evidence without treating them as substitutes
  for the randomization protocol.
- Align inference with assignment and address multiple outcomes or subgroups
  according to the approved analysis plan.

## Formal theory

- Define primitives, domains, equilibrium or solution concept, and notation
  before results.
- State assumptions where readers can see which result each licenses.
- Separate intuition from proof and distinguish existence, uniqueness,
  identification, and comparative-statics claims.
- Route proof verification to the repository's theory and deep-audit guidance;
  this drafting skill does not certify a proof.
